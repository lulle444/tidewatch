Why prices drift

Arbitrage keeps them close, most of the time

Robinhood mints and redeems stock tokens at the real share price, so when a token drifts far from it, traders can profit by closing the gap. Small gaps remain because of trading fees, pool depth and how fast traders react.

When the US market is closed for the weekend, share prices freeze but tokens keep trading. A gap then shows where traders expect the stock to open, not necessarily a bargain.

How we calculate

Two prices, one gap

Share price is the midpoint of Robinhood’s live bid and ask for one token, adjusted for dividends and splits. On chain is the price in the token’s deepest pool, as indexed by DexScreener.

Gap is the on-chain price divided by the share price, minus one. Within ±0.5% counts as fair. Pools under $10k are marked thin, because a single small trade can move them.
