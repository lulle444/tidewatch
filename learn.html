Yield guide

Understand yield before you chase it

The terms you’ll see across Tidewatch, explained in plain English, plus a short checklist to run before you deposit anywhere.

APY

Annual percentage yield

What you’d earn in a year if today’s rate held and earnings were reinvested. It’s a snapshot, not a promise: on-chain rates move every block.

Base vs. rewards

Where the yield comes from

Base yield is interest or trading fees paid by real users. Rewards are extra tokens a protocol hands out, and they can fall in value or stop at any time.

30-day average

The steadier number

A short spike can make any pool look great. The 30-day average shows what the pool has actually paid lately, so we use it in the calculator.

TVL

Total value locked

How much money sits in the pool. More TVL usually means deeper liquidity and a protocol that more people trust, but it’s not a safety guarantee.

Impermanent loss

The hidden cost of liquidity

When you provide two assets and their prices drift apart, you end up with less value than if you’d just held them. Big fees can offset it, but not always.

Lending vs. liquidity

Two ways to earn

Lending pays interest from borrowers and keeps your exposure to one asset. Liquidity pools earn trading fees but carry impermanent loss.

Stock tokens

Shares on-chain

ERC-20 tokens that track a real share and follow its price through a Chainlink feed. You can hold them, lend them out or pair them in a pool.

Audits

Reviewed, not bulletproof

An audit is an outside review of a protocol’s code. It catches many bugs but not all of them, and audited protocols have still been hacked.

Stablecoins

Stable, until they aren’t

Stablecoins aim to hold $1, but they depend on reserves or mechanisms that can fail. A stablecoin yield is only as safe as the coin itself.

Before you deposit

A five-point checklist

  1. Compare APY with the 30-day average. A big gap usually means a temporary spike.
  2. Check how much is rewards. Reward tokens can lose value faster than they pay out.
  3. Look at TVL and protocol age. Young, small pools carry more risk of bugs and exits.
  4. Know if you face impermanent loss. Single-asset lending avoids it; two-asset pools don’t.
  5. Size the position. Only deposit what you can afford to lose, and spread across protocols.
